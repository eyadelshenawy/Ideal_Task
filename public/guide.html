{{ICON:home}} What is IDEAL Tasks?

A shared task manager built for IDEAL for Digital Transformation — projects, tasks, deadlines, and who's doing what, all in one place, with email reminders so nothing falls through the cracks.

Every task belongs to a project, has one or more assignees (team members or outside contacts), a priority, a status, and dates. From there you can attach files, leave comments, check off subtasks, tag it, duplicate it, or set it to repeat automatically.

Support work is handled as tickets: each one has a type (Incident, Service Request…), an Impact × Urgency priority, a category, and — in SLA-tracked projects — a live countdown to its response and resolution deadlines. Customers can raise tickets through a public form, follow them on a tracking page, and rate the service once a ticket is closed.

Click What's New in the ⋯ menu (top right) any time for a quick summary of recently added features. The same menu holds every admin screen.

{{ICON:log-in}} Logging in

Accounts are created by a Super Admin from Team Management — there's no public sign-up.

The first time you log in, you'll be asked to set your own password (a temporary one is generated for you when your account is created). If your account is ever deactivated, you'll see a message here instead of the app.

If you've turned on two-factor sign-in (see Account security below), the app asks for the 6-digit code from your Authenticator app after your password. Tick Trust this device for 30 days to skip the code on that browser for a month.

Forgot your password?

Click Forgot password? on the login screen and enter your email — if an account exists for it, a reset link is sent that's valid for 1 hour. You don't need an admin for this anymore.

Too many wrong attempts locks the account for 2 minutes. After 5 incorrect attempts in a row — wrong passwords and wrong two-factor codes both count — further tries (even with the correct details) are blocked for 2 minutes as a brute-force protection. The counter resets on the next successful login.
Outside working hours, the first load can take up to a minute. The app is kept ready Sunday–Thursday, 8:45am–6pm Cairo time. In the evening, at night, and on Friday and Saturday it rests when no one is using it, so the first person to open it waits 30–60 seconds while it wakes up — after that it's fast as usual. Nothing is wrong; just wait for the page to load.

{{ICON:lock}} Account security

Three protections apply to every account: an optional second step at sign-in, automatic sign-out when you walk away, and password rules.

Two-factor sign-in (MFA)

Open the ⋯ menu → Two-factor (MFA) → Enable two-factor. Scan the QR code with an Authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, 1Password…) — or type the secret shown under it — then enter the 6-digit code the app shows to confirm. From then on, signing in asks for a fresh code after your password. Your account stays safe even if your password leaks.

To switch it off, open the same screen and confirm with your current password. Turning it on or off is recorded in the Audit Log.

Automatic sign-out

After 15 minutes with no activity the app signs you out, so an unattended screen doesn't stay open. One minute before, a warning appears with a Stay signed in button — click it (or just keep working) and the timer starts again.

Password rules

  • At least 8 characters, with an uppercase letter, a number and a symbol.
  • A new password can't be the same as the current one.
  • Passwords expire after 90 days — at your next sign-in after that you're asked to choose a new one before continuing.
Super Admins can change the sign-out time, the expiry period and the password rules in ⋯ menu → Security Policy (see Administration).

{{ICON:layout}} Board, List & Timeline views

Three ways to look at the same tasks — pick whichever fits what you're doing.

Board

The default view — tasks grouped into six columns: New, Ready, In Progress, Internal Test, Customer Test, Done. Drag and drop a card into a different column to change its status. On a phone or tablet, use the ‹ › arrows at the bottom of the card instead.

Each card shows the code on top, the full title, then the coloured tags that matter (type, SLA countdown, Blocked). Project, modules, category, tags and subtask info share one grey line underneath, and comment and checklist counts sit on the bottom line beside the due date. On a computer, the Edit, Duplicate and Trash icons appear when you move the mouse over a card.

A card shows small icons at a glance whenever it has any: a paperclip with a count for attachments, a speech bubble for comments, and a checklist icon showing done/total — so you know a task has activity without opening it.

Tickets also carry a short coloured type tag (INC, SR, CHG, PRB — plain tasks show none), and in SLA-tracked projects a countdown chip: "Respond in 2h", "Resolve in 1d 4h", or a red "SLA breached".

List

A flat, sortable table — better when you want to scan everything at once or sort by a specific column. Each row reads in one line: assignee, code, type tag and title, then project, modules, category, tags, subtasks and comment/file/checklist counts in grey. Status, the SLA countdown and the due date line up in columns on the right, and progress is the thin line along the bottom of the row. The Status pill is a drop-down — change a task's status right there, no need to open it or switch to Board. On a computer, the Edit, Duplicate and Trash icons appear when you move the mouse over a row.

Timeline

A Gantt-style calendar strip showing each task's span from start date to due date, so you can spot overlaps and clashes at a glance. Each bar shows how far the task has progressed, with the assignees' initials beside it. The view opens scrolled to today (the dotted red line); click Today to jump back after scrolling. Switch between Days and Weeks — Weeks shows one column per week, so a long project fits on one screen.

All three share the same counts at the top and toolbar (search, filters and sort — see the next section). Every drop-down list of people and projects is alphabetised.

{{ICON:filter}} Search, filters & saved filters

Narrow down what you're looking at, and keep your favorite combination one click away.

  • Search matches task title and code.
  • Filters: Assignees, Projects and Statuses sit in the toolbar; Priorities, Types, Categories and Modules are under More filters (a number on it shows how many of those are in use). Click one and tick as many values as you need — for example Critical and High together, or two projects at once; the list shows tasks matching any of them. The button shows the name when one value is picked, or a count when several are, and each list has a Clear link. Combine as many filters as you like.
  • The counts at the top are filters too. Click In Progress or Done at the top to show just those tasks (click again to clear); Total clears the status, Overdue and Milestones filters in one go.
  • Hide Done removes finished tasks from every view (Board, List, Timeline). It's switched on every time you open the app; turning it off only lasts until you leave. Picking Done in the Statuses filter shows Done tasks even while Hide Done is on.
  • Due this week is a single-click chip that hides everything not due in the next 7 days — the fastest way to see what actually needs your attention. Combines with any other filter above.
  • On a phone, the filter row collapses behind a single Filters button so the dashboard doesn't eat the screen — tap it to open every filter above, tap again to hide.
  • Sort: Due Date, Code, Priority, Assignee, or Newest first.
  • Save (right end of the toolbar) stores your current search + filter + sort combination (by name) so you can jump back to it later — it's saved per person, in your browser. Filters saved before multi-select arrived keep working.

Global search

Press Ctrl+K (or click {{IICON:search}} in the top bar) to open a search box that looks everywhere at once — task titles and codes, comment text, and attachment file names — not just the tasks currently on screen. Click any result to jump straight to that task.

Group (List view)

On large projects, the Group button in List view lets you stack tasks into collapsible sections by Project, Module, Assignee, or Priority — pick just one, or add several levels to nest them (e.g. Project, then Module inside each). A task with subtasks always stays together with its whole subtree in one group, even if a subtask's own field differs from its parent's. Leave it on "Group by" (no levels chosen) for the plain flat list you're used to.

Each group only lists its first 30 tasks at a time — click Show more to reveal the rest. This, plus a Compact toggle that shrinks row height, is what keeps a project with hundreds of tasks usable instead of one long scroll.

{{ICON:plus-circle}} Creating & editing a task

Click New Task, or the pencil on any existing task, to open the task form.

How the form is laid out

  • An existing task shows its title and code at the top, with tabs: Details, Checklist, Time, Files and Comments. A small number on a tab shows how many items it holds. A new task has only Details.
  • The first row of Details holds Status, Progress and, in SLA-tracked projects, the SLA tag — click the tag to see the response and resolve times.
  • Assignees are picked from a drop-down (Team, Inactive, External contacts) and shown as names; remove one with its ×.
  • More options (click to open) holds the less-used settings: Code, Tags, Milestone, Repeat, Depends on, Parent task, Duplicate and Add subtask.

Required fields when creating a task

  • Title
  • Description
  • Priority
  • Project
  • At least one assignee
  • Due Date — always required.
  • Start Date — required unless the task is a Milestone, in which case only one date (Due Date) applies, since a milestone is a single-day marker rather than a date range.

Ticket type, Impact × Urgency and categories

Every task has a Type. Task is the default, so project work carries on exactly as before. For support work pick Incident (something is broken), Service Request (someone needs something), Change Request or Problem (the root cause behind repeated incidents). Super Admins can rename these, change their tag and colour, hide ones you don't use, or add new ones in ⋯ menu → Lists.

Instead of guessing a priority, click pick (or change) next to Priority, then choose Impact (who is affected) and Urgency (how soon it's needed) and the priority fills itself in:

Impact ↓ / Urgency →HighMediumLow
High — the whole company or a critical processCriticalHighMedium
Medium — a department or teamHighMediumLow
Low — one personMediumLowLow

Rule: Priority = the cell where the Impact row meets the Urgency column. You can still change the priority by hand — the form then notes that it differs from what the matrix suggests.

Category and Subcategory (e.g. Bug / Error › Posting error) come from one shared list that Super Admins manage in ⋯ menu → Ticket Categories. Both are optional.

Priority (Critical, High, Medium, Low) and SLA auto-fill

Four priority levels — Critical (most urgent, red), High, Medium, Low. Sorting by Priority puts Critical first. In an SLA-tracked project (Manage Projects → Track SLA), picking a priority for a new task pre-fills the Duration field from that project's SLA resolution days, so the Due Date computes itself from your Start Date. Defaults: Critical 2 days, High 4 days, Medium 7 days, Low 10 days — tune them per project (Manage Projects → SLA targets) or org-wide (Reports → Edit default targets). Priority names and colours can be changed by a Super Admin in ⋯ menu → Lists; the four levels themselves are fixed because SLA targets depend on them.

The SLA tag on a ticket

In an SLA-tracked project, an open ticket shows an SLA tag at the top of the form; click it to open the SLA box with its Response by and Resolve by times and how much is left (or Met / Breached). The same countdown appears as a chip on the card and the row. If the project counts working hours only, the clock pauses outside working hours, weekends and holidays (see Work Calendar).

Priority change always rewrites Duration — escalating a Low ticket to Critical MUST tighten its deadline, so the SLA is re-applied even if you had typed a Duration manually. Typing your own Duration afterwards keeps it until you touch the priority again.

Parent-task rollup and dependency warnings

A task with subtasks (a parent) rolls its date range up from them: the earliest child Start becomes the parent's Start, the latest child Due becomes the parent's Due, and Duration is the working days between the two. You can still edit a parent's dates by hand, but the moment any child moves the parent snaps back to the recomputed span — the parent's dates aren't a fact of their own, they're a summary of the children.

If a task's Start Date lands before one of its predecessors (its Depends on list) will actually finish, it now shows a small ⚠️ Dep chip on the row and the card, with a tooltip naming the predecessor. The Task modal shows a banner spelling out which predecessor doesn't fit and suggesting you push Start back. The warning also cascades — anything further downstream of a slipping predecessor picks up the same chip, because it too will be delayed once the upstream slip hits.

Duration and the Work Calendar

Every non-milestone task also has a Duration field (in working days) next to Start Date and Due Date. Fill in any two of the three — the app computes the third on save, skipping weekends and any holidays configured in the Work Calendar. So "Start = 2026-09-06, Duration = 5" auto-fills the Due Date to the fifth working day; conversely "Start = 2026-09-06, Due = 2026-09-10" auto-fills Duration to 5. Editing Duration clears Due (and vice-versa) so the next save recomputes cleanly.

The Work Calendar lives in the admin menu (Super Admin only). It has two parts: a small toggle strip picking which weekdays count as working days (default: Sun–Thu on, Fri and Sat off), and a list of specific holiday dates your team observes (Eid, national holidays, company shutdowns). Both feed into every date calculation across the app — creating a task, editing one, importing Excel, cloning a project template. Everyone sees the same calendar; only Super Admins can change it.

Duration is the same permission tier as dates: only Super Admins and Project Admins can edit it. Everyone else still gets Status and Progress only.

Module (which SAP module or part of the system the task belongs to, e.g. FI, MM) is optional and shows as a small chip on the task. Pick it from the Add a module… drop-down; a task can have more than one (e.g. FI and CO) — remove one with its ×. The list itself is managed by Super Admins in ⋯ menu → Lists, so everyone uses the same spelling.

Editing an existing task never blocks you on the mandatory-field rules above — they only apply at creation, so older tasks that predate a rule are never locked out of being edited.

Project codes & task numbering

Each project has a short code (e.g. APEX), set once when the project is created. Every ticket gets the next number automatically when it's saved — APEX-0004, APEX-0005… — and a subtask gets its parent's code plus its own number (e.g. APEX-0001-03). The Code field shows the expected number greyed out; the final number is fixed on save, so two people saving at the same time never get the same one.

Once a ticket is created its code doesn't change. Only a Super Admin can type or change a code (it must still start with the project's prefix, e.g. APEX-). Because moving a ticket to another project, or under a different parent, changes its code, only a Super Admin can do that too. Need to record the customer's own reference number? Add it as a tag.

Depends on (predecessor tasks)

The predecessor list only shows tasks from the same project, with each one's code shown so it's easy to pick the right one. Checking a predecessor will fill in this task's Start Date from the predecessor's Due Date, but only if Start Date is still blank — it's a one-time suggestion, never something that overwrites a date you've already set.

Duplicating a task

The copy icon on any task creates a fresh copy — same title, assignees, tags, module, priority and dates, but a new code, status reset to New, and progress reset to 0%. From the task form (under More options) you can also duplicate straight into a different project. Clicking the copy icon doesn't duplicate right away — it turns into a checkmark for a second click to confirm, the same pattern as Move to Trash, so a stray click never creates an accidental copy.

{{ICON:git-branch}} Task hierarchy — parent tasks & subtasks

Break a big task down into real subtasks — each one a full task in its own right, with its own assignee, dates, and status — not just a checklist line.

How it works

Open the task form, click More options and pick a Parent task from the drop-down (only tasks in the same project are shown), or use + Add subtask under this task there. The subtask gets a hierarchical code off the parent's own code when saved — e.g. a parent ABC-0001 gets subtasks ABC-0001-01, ABC-0001-02, and so on. A subtask can itself have subtasks (ABC-0001-01-01), with no limit on how many levels deep.

The fastest way in: open the parent task itself and click + Add subtask under this task at the bottom of its form — it opens a fresh task already scoped to the right project, parent, and suggested code, so there's no hunting through the dropdown.

This is entirely optional — most tasks have no parent and no subtasks at all. Use it only where a task genuinely breaks down into a real sequence of sub-work.

The parent auto-completes — and reopens

Once every subtask under a parent is marked Done, the parent is automatically marked Done too (progress jumps to 100%). Reopen any one of those subtasks afterward, and the parent automatically reverts to In Progress, with its progress recalculated from however many subtasks are still done. You never have to remember to close out the parent yourself. A recurring subtask's next occurrence stays under the same parent, with a fresh code of its own.

Where it shows up

  • List view — the full picture: each subtask is nested directly beneath its parent, indented, with a collapse arrow to hide/show them.
  • Board view — since Board is split into status columns, a parent and its subtasks can land in different columns and can't literally nest there. Instead, a subtask shows "↳ parent code" on its grey line, and a parent shows "N/M subtasks". If a project has a lot of subtasks cluttering the board, toggle Hide subtasks in the filter bar to show only top-level tasks as cards (a parent's subtask-progress chip still reflects them either way).
  • Timeline — subtasks are indented under their parent the same way as List, regardless of status.

A few rules

  • A subtask always lives in the same project as its parent — you can't move a task with subtasks to a different project until they're moved or removed first.
  • A task can't become its own ancestor — the app blocks any change that would create a loop.
  • Moving a parent task to Trash takes its whole subtask tree with it; restoring the parent brings them all back too.

Importing a hierarchy from Excel

The import template has a Parent Code column. It can point at a code that already exists in the system, or at another row in the same file (handy for bringing in a brand-new project with its full breakdown structure in one upload) — leave a row's own Code blank and it gets a hierarchical code generated automatically, exactly like creating one by hand.

Using it for project phases

For a large new-implementation project organized into phases (e.g. Explore, Realize, Deploy, Run), there's no separate "phase" or "milestone" feature — just create one top-level task per phase, then add every other task as a subtask underneath the right one. Combined with Group by in List view, this gives you a clean phase-by-phase breakdown for a project with hundreds of tasks, using the exact same hierarchy described above.

Ongoing trackers (Master Data, Issue Log...)

The same trick handles something that doesn't belong to any one phase — a running Master Data list, an Issue Log, anything that sits alongside the phases rather than inside one of them. Create it as its own top-level task (e.g. XYZ-MD for Master Data, XYZ-IT for an Issue Tracker), and add each individual item as a subtask underneath (XYZ-MD-01, XYZ-MD-02...). It lives at the same level as the phase tasks, not nested inside any of them, and you can add or check off items at any point in the project's life.

{{ICON:list-checks}} Checklist, attachments, tags & comments

Open any task and use the tabs at the top — Checklist, Time, Files and Comments — to reach its checklist, time log, attachments and activity.

Use Email customer (above the comments box) to send the task's assigned contact an email with the task code, title, a link to view status and reply, and your message — it also logs a comment on the task marked "to customer". The assigned contact's email (if any) is used automatically, and you can also type in one or more extra addresses by hand (comma-separated) under "Also send to" — handy for tasks with no contact on file yet, e.g. ones created internally rather than through the public intake form. You can attach as many files as you like (image, PDF, or Word) as long as each stays under 10MB and the combined total under 25MB — they're sent as real email attachments and also saved to the task's Attachments. If they reply from that tracking link, their reply appears as a comment marked "via tracking link" (with its own attachment if they included one) and notifies the task's assignees and every Super Admin. The tracking page itself shows the back-and-forth thread of these messages, so the customer sees what was said before they reply. Any attached file shows as a clickable download link in the thread, both here on the task and on the customer's tracking page. If a link ever gets forwarded to the wrong person, Super Admins can hit Reset link in the Email customer box to invalidate the old link on the spot — then send a fresh email so the customer gets the new one.

Custom Fields

Shown on the Details tab, only when the task's project has custom fields defined (see Managing projects) — extra text, number, or dropdown fields specific to that project, e.g. a SAP transaction code. Saves as you fill them in.

Checklist

Lightweight subtasks — check them off as you go. They don't have their own assignee or due date; they're just steps within the task.

If a template exists (see Checklist Templates below), a small dropdown appears next to the Checklist heading — pick one to add its whole list of steps at once instead of typing them.

Time logged

A manual log, not a timer — enter how many hours you spent, on what date, with an optional note. Need to log a batch at once (a whole week that never got logged, or catching someone else up)? Open Reports and hit Log in bulk above the Time Logged section — one modal, one row per entry, submit all together. Super Admins and Project Managers can also log time on behalf of another team member (a drop-down appears next to the hours/date), and can edit or re-assign any existing entry — handy when someone forgot to log their own. Anyone who can see the task can log time on it; totals from everyone add up into one running total shown at the top of the section. You can remove your own entries any time; a Super Admin can remove anyone's.

Attachments

Attach any file up to 25MB. Files are stored securely and every download link is temporary (expires after a few minutes) — nothing is ever publicly accessible.

Tags

Free-form labels, under More options in the task form — type a word and press Enter. Use them however makes sense to your team (e.g. onboarding, urgent, client-facing).

A handy convention for support projects: tag a ticket Cancelled, Duplicate, or Not Applicable before closing it as Done, so it stays searchable and distinguishable from a ticket that was actually resolved — no separate status needed, since a ticket the client raised counts as closed either way.

Same idea while a ticket is still open: if you're waiting on the client for more detail before you can call it Ready, leave it in New and tag it Needs Info (or similar) instead of hunting for a status that doesn't exist — a tag surfaces it without adding a column to the board.

Comments & Activity

A running log for the task — your own comments alongside automatic entries for every change (status moves, reassignment, date changes). Nothing is ever deleted from this log. The comment box is a full-size textarea with room for long text (specs, pasted logs, test notes) — press Ctrl/Cmd + Enter to post, or use the Post button.

Type @ followed by a team member's full name to mention them — they get both an in-app notification and an email with a preview of your comment. Made a typo? Click the pencil icon next to your own comment to edit it in place; a small (edited) label appears afterward so nothing changes silently. Super Admins can edit anyone's comment, for moderation.

Recurring tasks

Set a task to repeat Daily, Weekly, or Monthly. The moment it's marked Done, the next occurrence is created automatically with the same details and a due date one interval later — until an optional end date is reached. Duration carries over unchanged, so a weekly 3-day task stays 3 days on every occurrence, and if the recurring task lives under a parent, the parent's rolled-up date range extends to cover the new occurrence.

{{ICON:check-square}} Selecting multiple tasks at once

Click the checkbox icon in the top toolbar to enter select mode, tick the tasks you want, then act on all of them together — set status, assignee, or project directly on the bar, or hit Mark Done as a shortcut. Behind More… you'll find priority, start date, due date, duration, progress %, module (picked from the Modules list), and add/remove a tag, one field at a time — dates flow through the same Duration ↔ Due auto-compute as the single-task modal, so bulk-setting a Duration of 5 on ten tasks recomputes each of their Due Dates against its own Start. Move to Trash deletes the selection (with a confirm step). And when you're in Trash, tick multiple tasks and hit Restore N selected to bring them all back at once.

Anyone can bulk-change the status of their own assigned tasks this way. Bulk-changing assignees or moving tasks to another project still requires the same admin rights as editing a single task normally would.

{{ICON:bar-chart}} Reports

A live snapshot, scoped to whatever you're allowed to see.

SLA

Response/resolution targets only make sense for support/ticket work measured from when a request came in — an implementation project's tasks already have their own Start/Due dates, so SLA is off by default for every project. Turn it on per-project from Manage Projects (see below) for the ones that are actually support contracts; the rest are left out of this section entirely.

Two targets are tracked automatically, by priority, for any project with tracking on — how quickly someone first commented on a task, and how quickly it was actually closed. Every project shares the same numbers by default (Critical: first response within 4 hours, close within 2 days; High: 24 hours / 4 days; Medium: 48 hours / 7 days; Low: 72 hours / 10 days) — a Super Admin can change these shared defaults via Edit default targets, or set different numbers for one specific project via its own SLA targets button in Manage Projects. Either level can also set a cutoff date so tasks created before it (e.g. old backlog imported before this feature existed) are left out of the percentages entirely.

Formula: SLA met % = tickets that met the target ÷ (tickets that met it + tickets that breached it) × 100, rounded to a whole number. The percentages only count tasks that have a definite outcome yet (met or breached) — a task still comfortably inside its window doesn't count against either side until it's actually late. The list below it names every task currently breaching either target, so you know exactly what needs attention.

Customer satisfaction

When a ticket in an SLA-tracked project is marked Done for the first time, and it has an outside contact with an email address, that contact gets one email asking them to rate how it was handled from 1 to 5 stars. One click on a star records the answer; they can add a comment on the page that opens. The rating shows in the ticket's activity, and its assignees get a notification. It's never sent twice for the same ticket.

This section of Reports shows the average score, the response rate, the average per project, and the latest comments.

Formulas used:

  • Average rating = sum of all ratings ÷ number of ratings received, rounded to one decimal place
  • Responses = surveys answered “of” surveys sent (e.g. 2 of 3)
  • Per-project average = sum of that project's ratings ÷ that project's number of ratings

Team Workload

Every active team member's open (non-Done) task count, sorted heaviest-first, with overdue and High-priority counts called out — a quick read on who's stretched thin and who has room for more. A task with more than one assignee counts toward each of them.

Time Logged

Total hours each person logged (see Time logged on a task) within any date range you pick — defaults to the last 7 days, but the two date fields are fully open, so a week, a month, or any custom range works. Useful for billing a support contract by the hour.

{{ICON:alert-triangle}} Needs Attention

Everything that's overdue, plus anything high-priority due within 3 days — the two lists most worth checking first thing.

{{ICON:bell}} The notification bell

In-app alerts for anything relevant to you — a new assignment, or a comment on a task you're on.

The same alerts also arrive as a push notification — to your phone or desktop even when the app isn't open in a tab. No separate button: your browser asks for permission once, automatically, the first time you open the app; allow it and you're set.

{{ICON:users}} Managing the team

Super Admin only. Open the ⋯ menu → Manage Team.

  • Name and email are both editable in place — click into either field and edit directly, it saves when you click away. Handy for a typo'd name or moving someone to a new email address entirely.
  • Toggle Inactive instead of deleting — it's reversible, and their past tasks stay exactly as they were.
  • The shield marks Super Admins.
  • The folder icon grants a member admin rights on specific projects.
  • The shield-off icon appears only for members who use two-factor sign-in. It switches two-factor off — for example when someone lost their phone — so they can sign in with their password and set it up again. Click twice to confirm; it's recorded in the Audit Log.
  • The trash icon permanently deletes the account — a step you can't undo, so Inactive is almost always the better choice.

{{ICON:clipboard-list}} Checklist Templates

Super Admin only. Open the ⋯ menu → Checklist Templates.

A named, reusable set of steps — e.g. "SAP PO ticket diagnosis" with five standard steps — that anyone can apply to any task's checklist in one click from that task's Checklist section, instead of typing the same steps out every time. Useful for recurring ticket types or a standard set of steps at the start of a project phase. Deleting a template doesn't remove steps already applied to a task's checklist — it only stops it from being offered for new ones.

{{ICON:folder}} Managing projects

Super Admin only. Open the ⋯ menu → Manage Projects.

Deleting a project moves it to Trash rather than removing it outright — its tasks are kept as-is and everything can be restored together.

Public status link

Click Create public status link under any project to get a link you can hand to a client or outside stakeholder — no login required. It opens with overall completion and an All / Open / Done breakdown they can tap to filter the list. Clicking any ticket opens its detail: description, attachments, and a conversation area where the client can add their own comment — they type their name once (remembered for next time) and send. Only messages your team explicitly sent via Email customer and the client's own replies appear in that thread; every internal comment your team writes on a ticket stays hidden from the share link. Revoke kills the old link immediately; if it ends up in the wrong hands, revoke then create — the new link is different, not the same one back. The link itself is short (a dozen characters after /share/), and the page can be added to a phone's home screen or a desktop as its own icon — opening it directly to that project's status, not the internal app.

Tapping any task opens its full details — description, comments, and attachments included, since it's their own task. The one thing that never shows, at either level: who on the team is behind it — no assignee or comment-author names, ever.

Ticket submission link

Click {{IICON:inbox}} Create ticket submission link under any project to get a separate public link where anyone — no login required — describes the issue, says what they need (Something isn't working becomes an Incident, I need something a Service Request), who is affected and how urgent it is, and optionally attaches a screenshot or file (image, PDF, or Word — up to 10MB). The app works out the priority from those answers using the Impact × Urgency matrix, in plain words the customer understands. It becomes a ticket in that project (New status) with the submitter added as an external contact, and every Super Admin gets emailed and notified in-app. This is a second, independent link from the status link above — revoking one doesn't affect the other. It's short and can be added to a phone's home screen or a desktop as its own icon, opening straight to the form.

After submitting, the visitor gets a private tracking link — a read-only page showing just that one ticket's current status, so they can check back later without an account. It's separate from the project's own status link (see above), which shows every task. Like the other public links, it's short and can be added to a home screen or desktop as its own icon. The page also has a reply box, so the customer can respond without an account — their reply lands as a comment on the task and notifies the assignees and every Super Admin.

Use as template

Click Use as template under any project to clone its entire task structure — including subtasks — into a brand-new project. Give the copy a name, a code, and a new start date; every task's dates shift by working days using the org's Work Calendar (so a task cloned during Eid week gets pushed past the holidays automatically), and Duration is preserved on each row. Task codes carry the source's readable suffix onto the new prefix — ONBOARD-W1 becomes ADEL-W1 under a project coded ADEL — and Depends On links survive the clone, remapped to the new tasks, so a template's sequencing keeps working on every hire. The per-project SLA targets (if the source has any) copy across too, so a Support-project template doesn't quietly reset to org defaults. Assignees, comments, attachments, and time logs are deliberately left behind — a clone is a fresh structural skeleton (handy for a repeatable phase breakdown like Explore/Realize/Deploy/Run, or an Onboarding/Training program), not a snapshot of one engagement's history.

Keeping the template out of the way. If a project exists only as a shape to clone from — never worked on directly — hit Mark as template under it. It (and every task in it) disappears from the main Dashboard and only shows up in the dedicated Templates section inside Manage Projects, where you can clone from it or restore it back to active later.

Client report

Click Client report under any project to open a one-page, print-ready status snapshot — overall completion, open/done/overdue counts, and (for SLA-tracked projects) a response/resolution breakdown. It opens in a new tab; use your browser's print dialog and choose "Save as PDF" to get a file you can email a client. Unlike the public status link, this page requires being signed in — it's a snapshot you generate and send yourself, not a standing link.

Custom fields

Click {{IICON:sliders-horizontal}} Custom fields under any project to add extra fields specific to that project only — text, number, or a dropdown of choices (e.g. a SAP transaction code). Once added, every task in that project shows the field in its own Custom Fields section. Fields aren't shared across projects — each project keeps its own set.

Track SLA

Tick Track SLA under a project to include it in the SLA section of Reports — meant for support/ticket-style projects, not implementation projects with their own scheduled dates. Once on, an {{IICON:timer}} SLA targets button appears for that project so you can give it its own response/resolution numbers instead of the shared default (see Reports → SLA).

Next to it, Working hours only decides how that project's SLA clock runs: unticked, it runs 24/7; ticked, it only counts working hours on working days (set in Work Calendar), so a ticket raised on Thursday evening doesn't breach over the weekend.

A ticket's assignees also get a one-time warning (email and in-app) once it enters the last quarter of its response or resolution time — before the deadline is missed, not after. This is checked every 15 minutes during working hours, so even a 4-hour Critical response target gets its warning in time.

{{ICON:user-plus}} Managing contacts

Contacts are external people — no login, no account — that you can still assign tasks to.

Each contact belongs to one project — pick it from the dropdown next to the contact's name. That way a client's people only show up as assignable options on their own project's tasks, not everyone else's. A contact submitted through a ticket intake form is scoped to that project automatically.

{{ICON:scroll}} Audit log

Super Admin only — a running history of sign-ins, team and project changes, security changes (two-factor on/off, password changes, Security Policy), changes to Lists and Ticket Categories, and tickets moved to or restored from Trash. Per-task activity lives on each task instead (see Comments & Activity above).

Use the search box, the person filter and the date range to narrow it down; the count above the list shows how many entries match. Entries older than 12 months are removed automatically each month.

{{ICON:trash}} Trash & restore

Deleted tasks and projects land here first, not gone for good.

{{ICON:folder}} Ticket Categories

Super Admin only. Open the ⋯ menu → Ticket Categories.

Rename with the pencil, add with the row at the bottom, and click a category to manage its subcategories (one level deep). Deleting a category asks for a second click to confirm, removes its subcategories too, and clears it from tickets that used it — nothing else on those tickets changes.

{{ICON:list-checks}} Lists — modules, ticket types and priorities

Super Admin only. Open the ⋯ menu → Lists. Change these lists here whenever you need to — no code changes required.

Modules

The list started from every module already typed on a task, so review it once and tidy it up. Each row shows how many tasks use the module.

  • Rename (pencil) — every task using the module changes with it.
  • Merge — rename one to a name already in the list (e.g. Fi → FI). The screen warns you first; click ✓ again to confirm. Its tasks move to the other entry and the duplicate disappears.
  • Hide (eye) — removes it from the drop-downs. Tasks that already have it keep it, and it stays in the Modules filter while any task uses it.
  • Add — type a new name in the bottom row.

Ticket Types

Rename a type, change its short tag (e.g. INC) and colour, hide it, or add a new one. Task, Incident and Service Request can't be hidden because the app itself relies on them (the default type and the customer ticket form).

Priorities

Change the name shown and the colour of each level. The four levels stay fixed because SLA targets depend on them.

Hiding never changes existing tickets. Excel import matches modules against this list — unknown ones are reported in the preview and left empty. Every change here is recorded in the Audit Log.

{{ICON:clipboard-list}} Work Calendar

Open the ⋯ menu → Work Calendar. Everyone can view it; only Super Admins can change it.

  • Work week — which weekdays count as working days (default Sunday–Thursday).
  • Working hours and time zone — used by projects whose SLA counts working hours only.
  • Holidays — specific dates your team observes (Eid, national holidays, company shutdowns).

All of these feed every date calculation in the app: Duration ↔ Due Date, Excel import, cloning a template, and working-hours SLA clocks.

{{ICON:shield}} Security Policy

Super Admin only. Open the ⋯ menu → Security Policy.

  • Auto-logout after inactivity — minutes (default 15; 0 switches it off).
  • Expire passwords after — days (default 90; 0 means never).
  • Minimum length and whether a password needs an uppercase letter, a number and a symbol.

Changes apply to every account at its next sign-in or password change, and are recorded in the Audit Log.

{{ICON:file-spreadsheet}} Importing & exporting Excel

Bring in a batch of tasks from a spreadsheet, or pull everything out into one.

  • Download template gives you a blank spreadsheet with every column already in place — Code, Title, Description, Module, Tags, Comment, Project, Assignee, Type, Category, Subcategory, Priority, Status, Start Date, Duration, Due Date, Progress, Milestone, Depends On, Parent Code. Fill in whichever ones you actually need; the rest can stay empty.
  • Import from Excel previews what will be created before committing anything — new projects mentioned in the sheet are created automatically, with a derived project code. Rows whose Project column is blank surface a picker in the preview (choose an existing project, or explicitly "Leave without a project") so nothing sneaks in unassigned. Rows whose Code doesn't start with the project's own prefix show a warning per row — the row still imports, but you see the mismatch up front. Type, Category, Subcategory and Module are matched by name against the configured lists; anything not found is listed as a warning and left empty. Files must be .xlsx or .csv — save older .xls files as .xlsx first.
  • Export to Excel pulls everything currently visible to you (respecting your role's normal visibility) into a spreadsheet, mirroring the import template's columns, plus Impact and Urgency — so an export can be imported straight back.

Duration on import

If a row supplies a Start Date and a Duration (in working days) but leaves Due Date blank, the app fills the Due Date in on commit using the org's Work Calendar — same auto-compute the single-task modal uses. Handy for templates: you can express "Week 1 is five working days from Sun 6 Sep" without hard-coding a Thursday date that stops being right the moment a holiday lands on that week.

The Comment column

If filled in, it becomes that task's very first comment on import (posted by you, the importer) — a quick way to carry over context from wherever the spreadsheet came from. It's not limited to a short note; paste as much as you need.

Task codes on imported rows are always generated fresh by the app's own numbering system, rather than trusting whatever code was written in the spreadsheet — keeping the same "automatic system" used everywhere else.

{{ICON:lock}} My Tasks — private personal tasks

A completely separate space for your own personal to-dos, kept apart from every company project.

Click My Tasks in the top bar to open it. Tasks you create here are visible only to you — not other team members, not other Project Admins, and not even other Super Admins. They never appear in Board, List, Timeline, Reports, the Needs Attention view, Excel export, or any email reminder — the only place they ever show up is this one tab, for the person who created them.

This is enforced on the server, not just hidden in the interface — even a direct attempt to open one of your personal tasks by another account is rejected as "not found," the same way a task you truly can't see behaves everywhere else in the app.

What's different from a regular task

  • No project, no assignees to pick — it's always just you.
  • A simpler form: title, description, priority, dates, status, and progress. No code, module, dependencies, subtasks, or recurrence.
  • Still gets its own checklist, file attachments, and comment log, exactly like a regular task.
  • Its own mini Board (New → Ready → In Progress → Internal Test → Customer Test → Done), fully separate from your company boards.

{{ICON:smartphone}} Installing IDEAL Tasks as an app

IDEAL Tasks is a Progressive Web App — you can install it like a native app on desktop or phone, no app store required.

On Windows or Mac (Chrome / Edge)

  1. Open the site in Chrome or Edge on your computer.
  2. At the right edge of the address bar, click the small install icon (a monitor with a down arrow). If you don't see it, open the browser menu ⋮ and pick Install IDEAL Tasks… (Chrome) or Apps → Install this site as an app (Edge).
  3. Confirm — the app opens in its own window (no browser tabs or URL bar) and gets a shortcut on your Desktop and Start menu / Applications folder, so you can pin it to the taskbar like any other program.

On Android (Chrome)

  1. Open the site in Chrome.
  2. Tap the ⋮ menu → Install app (or Add to Home screen).
  3. Confirm — it now opens full-screen from your home screen, with its own icon.

On iPhone (Safari)

  1. Open the site in Safari.
  2. Tap the Share icon → Add to Home Screen.
  3. Confirm — same result: a full-screen app icon on your home screen.
The installed app still talks to the same server — it's a shortcut and an offline-friendly shell, not a separate copy of your data. Uninstalling is the same as any other app on that OS (right-click the desktop icon on Windows, or open the app menu and pick Uninstall).

{{ICON:help-circle}} Frequently asked questions

I deleted a task by mistake — is it gone?
No — open the ⋯ menu → Trash. Deleted tasks and projects sit there for 30 days before they're removed for good, and can be restored any time before that.
Why can't I edit a task's title or dates, only its status?
You're not a Super Admin or a Project Admin on that particular project. Anyone assigned to a task can move its status and update progress; changing everything else (title, dates, assignees, project) needs admin rights on that project.
Can I move a task to a different project myself?
Only if you're a Super Admin, or a Project Admin on both the task's current project and the one you're moving it to.
Does changing a task's due date resend the "due tomorrow" email?
Yes — moving the due date resets that reminder, so it will fire again once the new date is one day away.
What happens to a recurring task's history when the next one is created?
The new occurrence is a separate task with a fresh code — it doesn't inherit the old one's comments, checklist, or attachments, only the same title, project, assignees, priority, and a due date one interval later.
Who can see the Audit Log?
Super Admins only. It's a system-wide history (logins, team/project changes) — separate from a task's own Comments & Activity log, which anyone who can open that task can see.
I got locked out after typing my password wrong — what now?
Wait 2 minutes and try again — the lock clears itself automatically. If you're not sure of your password at all, use Forgot password? on the login screen instead of continuing to guess.
Why did the app take a minute to open in the evening or at the weekend?
Outside working hours (Sunday–Thursday, 8:45am–6pm Cairo time) the app rests when no one is using it. The first visit wakes it up, which takes 30–60 seconds; everyone after that gets the normal speed. Just wait for the page — there's no need to refresh or report it.
I lost the phone with my Authenticator app — how do I sign in?
If you still have a trusted device (one you ticked “Trust this device for 30 days” on), sign in there, switch two-factor off, then set it up again on your new phone. Otherwise ask a Super Admin: in Manage Team, the shield-off icon next to your name switches two-factor off (click it twice to confirm). You then sign in with your password alone and can set it up again on the new phone.
Why did the app sign me out?
It signs you out after 15 minutes without activity (a Super Admin can change this in Security Policy). You get a one-minute warning with a “Stay signed in” button first.
A module I need isn't in the drop-down — what do I do?
Ask a Super Admin to add it in ⋯ menu → Lists → Modules. Keeping one shared list means everyone uses the same spelling, so filters and reports stay accurate.
